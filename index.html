about

I learned marketing fundamentals in a highly regulated industry: insurance. My work spans LinkedIn content strategy, SEO, AEO, and weekly reporting across submissions, campaigns, and content performance, the fundamentals that make strategy actually work. Insurance doesn't hand you creative freedom, you have to find it, and that search sharpened everything I do now.

I taught myself video production with a phone and a rigged podium, and I pitched a company president on a tool I found on LinkedIn. I thrive in the uncomfortable, because that's where I've grown the most. I'm not chasing one industry. I'm chasing the kind of work where a brand's story actually needs telling, and I want to find out how far that rigor travels.

Last month Urban Outfitters flew almost 200 community members out to AutoCamp Joshua Tree for the first season of Meet Me@UO, and I was one of them. Three days of desert hikes, brand partner workshops, and a surprise set from Rebecca Black. What stuck with me wasn't the lineup, it was the strategy behind it. Brand trips usually go to influencers with huge followings. UO bet on the opposite: people who already loved the brand and just needed a platform. That's a harder call to make and a smarter one, and watching it work from the inside is part of why I want to build brand experiences like it.

selected work

01 · problem

Gateway's first scripted video series, a "meet the team" introduction to the company's product directors, was fully produced and then shelved. Leadership only realized, after seeing the finished product, that there was no clear strategy behind it.

02 · solution

Rather than abandon video, the team pivoted to a lower-risk approach: repurposing existing webinar recordings into short, publishable clips. I assisted with production and polish, using Descript to clean up the audio, select the most engaging footage, and apply consistent branding so each clip was ready to publish.

03 · outcome

The pivot worked. The earliest clips used an audio/waveform format and averaged 6.1% engagement. Once the team shifted to using actual video footage, engagement rose to 9.4%. Comparing this against Gateway's employee culture videos (16.4% average) revealed a sharper pattern: content built around real people and candid moments consistently outperforms purely informational content, a finding now shaping the plan for Gateway's October webinar clips.

01 · problem

Gateway had minimal digital visibility beyond a basic company presence, and buyer research was shifting toward AI platforms (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity) rather than traditional search, a shift most of the industry, including Gateway, hadn't caught up to.

02 · solution

After connecting a trend I noticed on LinkedIn to a shift already visible in Gateway's own submission data, I researched HubSpot's AEO product, built the business case, and paired it with a second initiative: launching Gateway's first Instagram presence. I took direct ownership of LinkedIn content strategy, documented a repeatable process, and ran real tests comparing posting days and content types to improve performance. The same forward-looking instinct led me to research TikTok as a future channel, which I pitched directly to Gateway's VP.

03 · outcome

LinkedIn followers grew from 2,261 to 2,965, a 31% increase, under my direct ownership (prior growth was nearly flat). A/B testing showed Saturday posts earn 81% higher engagement than weekday posts, and that posts featuring real employees ("Community Spotlight") earn 432% higher engagement than standard product content, insights now built into the ongoing content strategy. Instagram launched October 1. The TikTok research was well received by leadership and is being brought to Gateway's president for consideration, still in idea phase.
